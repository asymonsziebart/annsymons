THE ANGLE QUEST

A Minecraft-style guide to beating your geometry homework

▶ Play the game  ·  Grown-ups (answer key)

Welcome, player!

Angles are just like building in Minecraft. Every block has perfect corners, and every flat floor is perfectly straight. If you can build a house, you can do this.

This guide teaches the rules. The game has extra build levels with pictures like your worksheet: crossed paths, flat ground, block corners, crafting, and congruence statements. Let's go! ⛏️

LEVEL 1: What is an angle?

An angle is how wide two lines open up from one point.

Think about opening a chest. A little crack open is a small angle. Wide open is a big angle.

We measure angles in degrees, written with a tiny circle: °. So 90 degrees is written 90°.

The point where the two lines meet is called the vertex. Think of it as the spawn point of the angle. Everything starts from there.

The two magic numbers

You only need to remember two numbers for almost this whole unit:

NumberWhat it looks likeMinecraft way to remember
90°A perfect corner. Your worksheet shows it with a tiny square box ⬜.The corner of any block
180°A perfectly straight lineFlat ground. Or turning around to face backwards.

Every angle gets one of four names. To check, compare it to the corner of a block. That corner is 90°.

LEVEL 3: The 3 Big Rules

These 3 rules unlock almost every problem. Learn these and you're basically in diamond armor.

⚔️ Rule 1: The Crossed Swords rule (vertical angles)

When two lines cross, they make an X like two crossed swords.

When two lines cross, the angles across from each other are twins. They are exactly the same size.

  • Blue and blue are the same. 🔵 = 🔵
  • Orange and orange are the same. 🟠 = 🟠

Across the X = SAME. So you set them equal: ____ = ____

Their math name is vertical angles.

🟫 Rule 2: The Flat Ground rule (linear pair)

Two angles sit side by side on flat ground (a straight line).

Together they make the whole flat ground. Flat ground is 180°. So:

Side by side on a straight line = ADD UP TO 180

It's like splitting a 180-piece stack between two chests. Whatever's in one chest, the rest goes in the other.

Their math name is a linear pair.

🧱 Rule 3: The Block Corner rule (complementary)

See the tiny square box? That means a block corner, 90°. If a line splits that corner into two pieces:

Two pieces of a block corner = ADD UP TO 90

➕ Bonus Rule: The Crafting rule (angle addition)

In Minecraft, small pieces craft into one big item. Angles work the same way. Little angles fit together to make the big angle.

piece + piece = whole thing

If the problem gives you the whole and one piece, just subtract: whole − piece = the other piece.

Example: the whole angle is 110° and one piece is 45°. The other piece is 110 − 45 = 65°.

🎯 Bonus Rule: Matching marks

Sometimes two angles have the same little curved mark on them, like ⌒ and ⌒. That's a secret code. It means "these two are the same size." So set them equal, just like crossed swords.

LEVEL 4: Which rule do I use?

Look at the two angles and ask yourself these questions in order:

QuestionIf YES...
1. Do they have matching marks?They're the same. Set them equal.
2. Are they across the X from each other, touching only at the middle point?Crossed swords. Set them equal.
3. Are they side by side on a straight line?Flat ground. Add them = 180.
4. Are they inside a corner with the square box?Block corner. Add them = 90.
5. Are they pieces of a bigger angle whose size they gave you?Crafting. Add the pieces = the whole.
💡 Finger trick: put your finger on one angle and slide it to the other one.
👉 If you have to jump over the middle point, they're crossed swords, so they're the same.
👉 If you only step over one line, they're on flat ground, so they add to 180.
✅ Look check: crossed-sword twins always look the same size. Flat-ground buddies usually look different: one skinny and one wide.

LEVEL 5: Solving for x (the mining part ⛏️)

x is a hidden ore. You have to dig it out.

An equation is like a trade with a villager. Both sides have to stay fair. So whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other side too.

To dig out x, you undo things with the opposite move:

If you see...Do the opposite to BOTH sides
+ 10take away 10 (− 10)
− 10add 10 (+ 10)
3x (3 times x)divide by 3 (÷ 3)
x on both sidestake away the smaller x-amount from both sides

Example 1: Crossed swords

Two lines cross. One angle is (5x + 10). The angle across from it is (3x + 30). Which rule? They're across the X, so they're the SAME. 5x + 10 = 3x + 30 Step 1: get the x's together. Take away 3x from both sides. 2x + 10 = 30 Step 2: undo the +10. Take away 10 from both sides. 2x = 20 Step 3: undo the "2 times". Divide both sides by 2. x = 10 ⛏️ Ore found! Step 4: put 10 back in where x was. 5(10) + 10 = 50 + 10 = 60° 3(10) + 30 = 30 + 30 = 60° Both are 60°. Twins! ✅

Example 2: Flat ground

Two angles are side by side on a straight line: (4x + 20) and (2x + 40). Which rule? Flat ground, so they ADD to 180. 4x + 20 + 2x + 40 = 180 Step 1: put the like things together. x's: 4x + 2x = 6x numbers: 20 + 40 = 60 6x + 60 = 180 Step 2: undo the +60. Take away 60 from both sides. 6x = 120 Step 3: undo the "6 times". Divide by 6. x = 20 ⛏️ Step 4: put 20 back in. 4(20) + 20 = 100° 2(20) + 40 = 80° Check: 100 + 80 = 180 ✅

Example 3: Block corner

A corner with the square box is split into (2x + 5) and (x + 10). Which rule? Block corner, so they ADD to 90. 2x + 5 + x + 10 = 90 3x + 15 = 90 (2x + x = 3x, and 5 + 10 = 15) 3x = 75 (take away 15 from both sides) x = 25 (divide by 3) 2(25) + 5 = 55° and 25 + 10 = 35° Check: 55 + 35 = 90 ✅

Example 4: When there's a minus sign

Minus signs are where a lot of players take damage. Go slow here!

20 = 2x − 30 You see − 30, so do the opposite: ADD 30 to both sides. 20 + 30 = 2x 50 = 2x x = 25 ⚠️ Do NOT write −30 again on the other side. Adding 30 makes the −30 disappear and turns into +30 on the other side.

Example 5: "Find ALL the angles"

Once you know one angle in an X, you know all four:

  • The angle across from it is the same.
  • The angles next to it are 180 minus it.

Example: one angle is 70°. Across from it is also 70°. Next to it is 180 − 70 = 110°. So the four angles are 70°, 110°, 70°, 110°.

Example 6: There's a "y" too

Don't panic. Two-step quest:

  1. First find x and get the angle sizes, just like the examples above.
  2. Then use flat ground: all the angles on the straight line add to 180. So y = 180 − (the other angles).

Example: on a straight line you have y, 22°, and 22°. Then y = 180 − 22 − 22 = 136°.

LEVEL 6: Naming angles

Angles get names using letters, like a player tag.

  • The vertex (spawn point) letter always goes in the middle.
  • Say the angle has points S, T, U and the vertex is T. You can call it ∠STU or ∠UTS. Both work, because T is in the middle.
  • If there's only one angle at that point, you can just say ∠T.
  • If there's a number inside the angle, like 4, you can call it ∠4.

So that angle's 4 names are: ∠STU, ∠UTS, ∠T, ∠4.

The sides are the two lines coming out of the spawn point: ray TS and ray TU. A side always starts with the vertex letter.

CONGRUENCE STATEMENTS

Congruent means the same size. The sign is ≅ (an equals sign with a extra wavy line).

∠ABC ≅ ∠DEF

  • Read it: "angle A-B-C is congruent to angle D-E-F."
  • The middle letters are the vertices: B matches E.
  • The other letters match in the same order: A↔D and C↔F.
  • You may flip both names: ∠CBA ≅ ∠FED. The middles stay the vertices.

Matching tick marks on a picture mean the same thing as ≅. Then the measures are equal, so you can write an equation:

m∠ABC = m∠DEF

That is how a congruence statement turns into "set them equal" when you solve for x.

⚠️ CREEPER WARNINGS (common traps)

  • 💥 Minus sign mix-ups. When you move a number, use the opposite move. Write one step per line.
  • 💥 Stopping at x. If it says "find the angle," put x back in! x is the ore. The angle is the diamond you craft from it.
  • 💥 Negative answers. Angles can't be negative. If you get a negative, go back and check your steps.
  • 💥 Guessing a box. Only use 90 if you see the square box, or the problem says 90°.

🐉 BEFORE YOU TURN IT IN (Ender Dragon checklist)

  • Crossed swords: did both angles come out the same number?
  • Flat ground: do they add up to 180?
  • Block corner: do they add up to 90?
  • Crafting: do the pieces add up to the whole?
  • Does it look right? Skinny angles should be under 90. Wide ones should be over 90.
